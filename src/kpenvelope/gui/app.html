2. Results

Each interface between two layers carries a fixed electric charge, set by the two materials, how much they are stretched, and any dislocations. A positive charge pulls electrons to the interface (this is what makes the electron layer of a GaN transistor). A negative one pulls holes.

Solves for where the electrons sit, using quantum mechanics near the surface and simple statistics deeper down.

How sure is the electron density? Enter how well you know each input (one standard deviation). Leave a box at 0 if you know that number exactly.

Puts a metal on top and sweeps its voltage. Capacitance (C-V) shows how the electron layer empties as the voltage goes negative. Current (I-V) shows how much current flows from top to bottom.

C-V needs no transport numbers. The electrons stay at the Fermi level of the side contacts and only the gate moves; holes are left out, because a fast sweep cannot make them in a wide-gap material.

Layer stack
The layers grown on top of each other, listed from the top down here, with the bottom layer as the thick base (buffer).
Fraction
How much of the metal in an alloy is the named one. AlGaN with 0.25 means 25 percent aluminum, 75 percent gallium.
Relaxed
A thin layer on a different base is squeezed or stretched to fit it. Above some thickness it relaxes back toward its own size by forming dislocation lines. 0 percent means it fits the layer below exactly.
Interface charge
A fixed charge at the boundary between two layers. In nitrides it comes from polarization: the crystals are electrically polar, and the polarity changes from one layer to the next.
Electron sheet density
How many free electrons sit in the thin layer at an interface, per square centimeter. Transistors use these electrons.
Surface barrier
Where the energy levels sit at the top surface. It depends on the surface and on any metal there.
Error bar
The range the answer probably lies in (one standard deviation), given how well the inputs are known.
Capacitance-voltage (C-V)
A standard measurement: as the gate voltage goes more negative, electrons leave, and the capacitance drops. The voltage where it drops is the threshold.
What this app does not do
It has no current over or through barriers by tunneling, no heating, and no time dependence. It uses one conduction band for electrons. All of these are stated in the documentation.

Every built-in number names its source; see the "Charges" tab, and the documentation of kpenvelope.
